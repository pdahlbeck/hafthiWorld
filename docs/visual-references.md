# Gotland visual pass: evidence and interpretation

Sources consulted 2026-10-21. All meshes and colors in this scene are authored in Rust; no museum photographs, game assets or third-party textures are included.

- [SGU: Sveriges berggrund](https://www.sgu.se/om-geologi/berg/sveriges-berggrund/) identifies Gotland among the areas with a sedimentary cover. [SGU: karst on Gotland](https://www.sgu.se/om-sgu/nyheter/2022/april/sgu-har-undersokt-karst-pa-gotland/) describes carbonate bedrock, exposed rock and limestone layers. This supports the limestone material vocabulary. It does **not** establish the shoreline of any particular farm around 900 CE, or justify a medieval/modern quarry industry in the simulation.
- [Gotlands Museum: Midgård – Gotlands vikingar](https://www.gotlandsmuseum.se/utstallning/midgard/) emphasizes everyday environments, material culture and interaction with nature in a Gotland-specific Viking Age context. This is broad contextual support, **not** documentation for this house's dimensions, roof covering, joinery or farm layout.

The scene is a fictional, very small coastal clearing, not a reconstruction of a named site. The low irregular shore, planted-looking pine group, meadow grass density, simplified timber walls, pitched roof with a straw-colored covering, door placement and warm palette are artistic interpretations. The roof is not claimed as a verified Gotlandic building type from 900 CE. Archaeological plans and specialist evidence are still needed before claiming a specific regional building reconstruction. Present-day farm museums must not be treated as direct Viking Age evidence.

The shallow-water bands and broken wave lines are stylized color/depth cues, not bathymetry, tide simulation or a reconstruction of historical sea level. The sun's path, 12-minute day, readable night lighting and wind phase are presentation choices, not astronomical or meteorological models. Gameplay work durations, walking clearance and six-log yields remain technical choices. This PR adds no mining, agriculture, weather or household claims.

## First crop slice

The added field is an illustrative grain plot, not an identified archaeological field or a species-specific reconstruction. Its rectangular placement, rows, generic green/gold ears, sack shapes, four seed/yield units and 30-second growth interval are technical/artistic choices. No claim is made that these are Gotlandic crop yields, varieties, field organization or calendars around 900 CE. Farming-specific archaeological/botanical sources are required before adding such claims. The one-cycle prototype adds no livestock, seasonal calendar, diets or household reconstruction.

## First sheep slice

The roadmap's sheep-to-wool chain is represented by two generic stylized animals. No specific historic breed, modern Gotland sheep ancestry, stocking density, pasture requirement, shearing technology, yield or frequency is claimed. Grazing paths, the three-sided wooden pen, holding animals in place when ordered, the reduced shorn coat and two-unit fleece are gameplay/art interpretations. Four-second clipping is a technical demonstration, not a husbandry estimate. There is no simulated feed consumption, animal health, reproduction or wool regrowth yet. No third-party animal meshes or textures are used.

## First household supply slice

Home 1 is a single abstract ownership/demand unit attached to the existing worker and house, not a reconstruction of a named Gotland family, household size or land tenure system. The visible door-side pantry rack is a game affordance, not a documented Viking Age storage practice. Grain-to-ration conversion bypasses processing and does not claim that raw grain was eaten as a complete diet. Four grain units, forty-second ration intervals, delayed clock start and shortage counters are technical rules. No family population, nutrition, cooking, starvation deaths, seasons or additional household autonomy is asserted.


## Seed reservation and repeat crops

The one-sack-to-four-seed-units conversion, four-second sorting task, standing reservation priority, reusable sack slots and repeatable thirty-second crop growth are technical game abstractions. They are not measurements of medieval seed ratios, harvest frequency, agricultural labor or a Gotlandic seasonal calendar. The current scene does not depict threshing, drying, winnowing or seed selection in detail; the preparation task stands in for that future processing chain. No additional archaeological or botanical claim is introduced.


## First shore barter offer

The roadmap requests Baltic trade and imported salt; this slice depicts only a fictional visitor's brought-in stock. It adds no claim about a specific trade route, place of salt production, archaeological cargo assemblage, Gotlandic salt extraction or a historical wool/salt price. The 2:1 exchange, two available pouches, stationary visitor, blue tunic, pale merchandise sacks and cloth spread are illustrative game rules/art. Source-specific research is still required before adding named origins, documented exchange rates, a harbour reconstruction or vessel types. Salt has no food-processing effect yet. No external meshes, textiles or character art are used.


## First seasonal calendar and palette

Four equal 120-second seasons, spring/summer starts for a generic crop, winter growth suspension, survival of planted/ripe crops and unchanged winter walking/trade/grazing are deliberately simplified game rules. They are not dated sowing/harvest evidence, a species-specific growth model, a reconstruction of Viking Age Gotland's climate, or a claim that all crops could overwinter. Meadow/grass colors are original artistic cues rather than botanical surveys; evergreen pines retain their color. There is no snow/ice, precipitation or astronomical coupling to the separate presentation sun. Crop-specific archaeobotanical and climate research is still needed before making historical calendar claims.


## First winter firewood demand

The winter-demand step uses the existing generic timber units as a stand-in for prepared firewood. It makes no claim about log mass, splitting/drying, actual heat output, household fuel requirements or a specific Gotlandic hearth/chimney form. One log per forty winter seconds, a three-log top-up target, the small door-side rack and shortage-only consequences are gameplay/art abstractions. No hearth interior or live fire is reconstructed; warmth is represented only by fuel demand and inventories. Named archaeological building/hearth evidence and species-specific fuel research are still needed before making such historical claims.


## Coastal-food package: interpretation limits

The generic fish silhouettes, shore net-frame marker, table, two-fish/one-pouch recipe, finite four-fish allowance and short work durations are original game abstractions. They are not an identification of a historical species, fishing technology, seasonal yield, processing duration or a documented Gotlandic salting recipe circa 900 CE. Salt is existing brought-in trading stock, not local production; no named origin or route is added. Brown output denotes the implemented preserved-ration stage, not a chemical or food-safety model. There is no spoilage, cooking, nutrition or disease system, and omitting fresh fish as a ration is a scope choice rather than a historical dietary claim. Species/gear/diet-specific archaeology and processing evidence are required before making more specific regional claims. No external fish, net or furniture assets are used.


## Two-resident labor slice

The blue coastal helper, free shore-side invitation, fixed founder/coastal role split and unchanged per-household ration/fuel quotas are fictional gameplay choices. They do not establish a Viking Age Gotland household population, recruitment custom, gender/age role, wage system or historical labor productivity. Both residents belong to the existing abstract home. There is no claim of an independently reconstructed second farm, family demography or individual nutrition. The helper is an original procedural actor using the scene's existing material vocabulary.


## First outbound collection boat

This is an original procedural wooden-boat silhouette with tapered hull, visible plank-like strips, raised ends, mast and furled cloth. It is a gameplay symbol, not an archaeological reconstruction or a typological identification of a Gotland vessel circa 900 CE. No particular dimensions, construction method, sail rig, harbour layout, mooring arrangement, wool cargo, route or sailing duration is asserted as historical evidence. A fixed dry-land handover point and an authored boat route replace future boarding, seamanship and harbour work. Named vessel/site-specific research remains necessary before depicting a documented Gotlandic harbour or ship type. No external boat meshes, plans, photographs or textile assets are used.


## Visiting sailors and sail presentation

Two small visiting sailor models, steering oar and a curved plain square cloth make the existing boat's movement legible. These are original stylized geometry, not identified people, documented clothing, a named historical rig or evidence for a two-person crew on a particular Gotlandic cargo vessel. Three-second raising/lowering, fixed sailing speed and unmodeled wind/crew work are presentation/game abstractions. There are no copied museum plans or external character/sail assets. Specific vessel, rigging and crew evidence remains necessary before claiming a historical reconstruction.
